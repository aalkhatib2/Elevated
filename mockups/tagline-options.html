“Above all else”

Five directions for the line under the wordmark, each shown against the actual mark. The plate is cropped to its type area so the pairing is judged the way a visitor sees it.

01Cinzel

Engraved Roman capitals. Closest structural match to the wordmark, whose letters are Roman underneath the gothic weathering. Widest tracking of the five, so it reads as a considered subtitle rather than a second logo. Safest and most legible.

Above all else
02Grenze Gotisch

A condensed gothic that keeps blackletter character while staying readable at small sizes. The tonal twin of the wordmark: same family of shapes, dialled down so it supports rather than competes.

Above all else
03Pirata One

Full blackletter display weight. The most literal match to the mark's texture and the boldest of the set. Strong at large sizes; the heavy strokes start to fill in on small screens.

Above all else
04UnifrakturMaguntia

Authentic Fraktur, the most historically gothic option. Maximum atmosphere, lowest legibility: the capital A and the double-l are genuinely hard to parse at a glance. Worth seeing, hard to recommend for a line people need to read quickly.

Above all else
05Cormorant Garamond Italic

The deliberate counterpoint. Rather than echoing the gothic, it answers it with a light high-contrast italic serif. Reads as a motto or a signature, and gives the mark room to be the loud element. Pairs with the Veni Vidi Vici line already on the page.

Above all else
